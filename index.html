Why this exists

01

Fees are quoted monthly, decisions last for years

Families rarely see the full multi-year total before choosing a pathway — and by the time it's visible, the choice has usually already been made.

02

The real costs stay hidden until they're due

Private tutoring, transport, exam fees billed in US dollars, equivalence certificates — none of it shows up on the first fee card a family sees.

03

Comparisons run on reputation, not numbers

O-Levels, Matric, GED — the choice between them is usually word-of-mouth. AwaamEd puts real, sourced figures next to each other instead.

Why I'm building this

Between O-Levels, a period of financial uncertainty, and eventually the GED, I moved through more of Pakistan's education systems than most families ever compare directly — and kept asking the same question: how much of a student's path comes down to ability, and how much to a family's ability to pay.

Private schooling, tutoring, and alternative credentials have each grown into separate, largely hidden cost layers on top of tuition. AwaamEd exists to make that full cost visible and comparable, so a family's choice can be driven by fit — not by which costs happen to show up first.

— Ali Tariq, founder

What's coming next

  1. More cities. Karachi and Islamabad data collection begins next, following the same sourcing standard as Lahore.
  2. A scholarship and financial-aid directory, so a family can see funding options alongside the cost.
  3. A visualised tuition-inflation tracker, published on an annual cycle once enough years of data exist.
  4. Research and distribution partnerships — with organisations already doing rigorous education-cost research and public education-access campaigns in Pakistan — to get this in front of families directly, not just online.

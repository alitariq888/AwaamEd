About AwaamEd

Data & methodology

How the numbers on this site are collected, checked, and kept honest.

Sourcing standard

Every fee in the AwaamEd calculator carries one of three tags, shown next to the figure itself:

Collected a real fee card, receipt, or published exam-board schedule, with a source and date   Estimate a reasonable figure not yet confirmed against a real source   Yours a number a family has entered themselves, overriding ours

Nothing is presented as certain until it is Collected. Where a figure is still an Estimate, the calculator says so plainly rather than implying a precision it doesn't have — and any family can overwrite any number with their own.

Current coverage

Phase 1 is hyper-localised to Lahore, comparing four pathways: O/A-Levels through a school, O/A-Levels with a privately-sat A-Level, Matric/FSc, and the GED. The founder's own educational history across these routes forms the first dataset, cross-checked where possible against official schedules from British Council Pakistan, GED Testing Service, IBCC, and individual institutions.

How data is collected

Two methods feed the dataset. The first is direct fee collection from schools, colleges, and exam-registration centres — tuition, exam fees, and any published schedule, recorded with a source and date. The second is structured household interviews with families who have completed each pathway, covering monthly and one-off costs, decision-making, and any scholarships or aid received.

Interviews are framed around family budgeting and consumer transparency, not evaluation, so participation stays comfortable and honest. Every figure — from either method — is recorded with its source and date so it can be checked and updated later. The approach follows the same discipline as household education-cost surveys used in research programmes like LEAPS and RISE: dated, sourced, and reproducible, rather than anecdotal.

How the totals are calculated

The calculator projects each pathway's full cost using a small set of transparent assumptions, all adjustable in its Settings panel:

InflationEach year's cost is grown by a yearly fee-inflation rate before being added to the total.
Opportunity costOptionally, the income a student could have earned instead of studying, multiplied by the years in the pathway.
TransportDistance from home × two trips a day × school days × a per-km rate for the chosen mode (a school van is a flat monthly fee instead).
CurrencyExam fees quoted in US dollars (common for Cambridge, Edexcel, and GED fees) convert at an adjustable USD-to-PKR rate.

Status

Currently live for Lahore, with new cities in progress as of August 2026. The site is free to use, requires no account, and collects no personal data from visitors.

What's next

Phase 2 extends the dataset to more cities, adds a scholarship and financial-aid directory, and builds a visualised tuition-inflation tracker published on an annual cycle. It also seeks research partnerships with organisations already doing rigorous education-cost research in Pakistan, and distribution partnerships with organisations already running public education-access campaigns — so the numbers reach families directly, not just whoever finds the site.

Get in touch

Spotted a wrong figure, or have fee data to contribute? Open an issue on the project's GitHub repository — every correction moves a number from Estimate to Collected.
